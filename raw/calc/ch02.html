Limits

Introduction to Calculus

Calculus emerged from the need to solve practical problems in physics and engineering. Two fundamental problems led to its development:

  1. The Tangent Problem: Determining the slope of a line tangent to a curve at a given point.

  2. The Area Problem: Calculating the area under a curve.

The Tangent Problem and Differential Calculus

The rate of change is a critical concept in calculus. To understand this, consider the graphs of the following linear functions:

  • \( f(x) = -2x - 3 \)

  • \( g(x) = \frac{x}{2} + 1 \)

  • \( h(x) = 2 \)

Graphs of linear functions with constant rates of change.
Concept

The slope of a linear function indicates the rate of change. For example:

  • \( f(x) = -2x - 3 \): slope = -2

  • \( g(x) = \frac{x}{2} + 1 \): slope = 1/2

  • \( h(x) = 2 \): slope = 0

Now consider the quadratic function \( k(x) = x^2 \). Its rate of change is not constant. To approximate it, we use secant lines.

Graph of \( k(x) = x^2 \) with a secant line.
Definition

A secant line to a function \( f(x) \) through points \( (a, f(a)) \) and \( (x, f(x)) \) passes through these points. Its slope is \[ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}. \] As \( x \) approaches \( a \), the secant line approaches the tangent line. Its slope approaches the derivative of \( f(x) \) at \( a \).

Example 28

Find the slope of the secant line to \( k(x) = x^2 \) through points \( (1,1) \) and \( (2,4) \): \[ m_{\text{sec}} = \frac{4-1}{2-1} = 3. \]

Concept

The slope of the tangent line at a point gives the instantaneous rate of change, which is the derivative of the function at that point.

The Area Problem and Integral Calculus

To find the area under a curve, approximate by summing rectangles.

Approximation of the area under \( f(x) = x^2 + 1 \) using rectangles.
Definition

The area under a curve is approximated by summing the areas of rectangles over subintervals. As the width of the rectangles decreases, the sum approaches the exact area. This process leads to the definite integral.

Example 29

Estimate the area under \( f(x) = x^2 + 1 \) over \( [0,3] \) using three rectangles: \[ \text{Area} \approx \sum_{i=1}^{3} \text{Area of Rectangle}_i \]

Conclusion

The Tangent and Area Problems lead to the foundations of calculus:

  • Tangent Problem $\rightarrow$ Derivative (rate of change)

  • Area Problem $\rightarrow$ Integral (area under curve)

These concepts are essential for further studies in calculus and multivariable calculus.

Limits

Definition of a Limit

We say the limit of \(f(x)\) is \(L\) as \(x\) approaches \(a\) and write \[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \] provided we can make \(f(x)\) as close to \(L\) as we want for all \(x\) sufficiently close to \(a\), from both sides, without actually letting \(x=a\).

Intuitive Idea

The limit describes the behavior of \(f(x)\) around \(x=a\), not necessarily the value at \(x=a\). As \(x\) approaches \(a\), \(f(x)\) must move closer and closer to \(L\). This must hold for values of \(x\) on both sides of \(a\).

Estimating Limits

To estimate limits, we examine the behavior of a function as \(x\) approaches a point \(a\) from both sides. The idea is to find a value \(X\) such that whenever \(|x-a| < X\), the function's value \(f(x)\) stays within a desired tolerance of the limit \(L\).

Example 30

Estimate \[ \lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 + 5x - 18}{x^2 - 3x}. \]

Observation

Although the function is undefined at \(x=3\), we can study its behavior for values near 3.

Example 31

Define \[ g(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{x^2 + 5x - 18}{x^2 - 3x}, & x \neq 3\\ 7, & x=3 \end{cases} \] Estimate \(\lim_{x \to 3} g(x)\).

Answer: As in Example 1, the limit is 4, even though \(g(3) = 7\). This shows that limits depend on nearby values of \(x\), not the function's value at the point itself.

Example 32

Estimate \[ \lim_{\theta \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{\theta}. \]

Observation: The function is undefined at \(\theta = 0\), but by evaluating values close to 0, we can approximate the limit.

Example 33

Estimate \[ \lim_{t \to 0} \sin(\pi t/2). \]

Observation: Tables of values may help, but they can be misleading. Graphical analysis or algebraic methods provide more reliable estimates.

Example 34

Estimate \[ \lim_{t \to 0} X(t), \quad X(t) = \begin{cases} -1, & t<0\\ 2, & t \ge 0 \end{cases} \]

Observation: Not all limits exist. Here, the left-hand limit is \(-1\) and the right-hand limit is 2, so the limit does not exist.

Summary

Key Points

  • Limits describe the behavior of a function around a point, not necessarily the function's value at that point.

  • Limits can exist even if the function does not exist at that point.

  • A function can exist at a point, but its limit at that point might be different.

  • Tables and graphs can help understand limits but are not always reliable for exact values.

  • Some limits do not exist.

One-Sided Limits

In the previous section, we saw two limits that did not exist for different reasons:

  • \(\displaystyle \lim_{t \to 0} \cos(\pi t)\) does not exist because the function oscillates and does not settle to a single value near \(t=0\).

  • \(\displaystyle \lim_{t \to 0} X(t), \quad X(t)=\begin{cases}0,& t<0\\1,& t\ge 0\end{cases}\) does not exist because the left-hand and right-hand limits are different.

To distinguish these situations, we introduce one-sided limits.

Definitions: One-Sided Limits

Right-Hand Limit

We say \[ \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L \] if we can make \(f(x)\) as close to \(L\) as desired for all \(x>a\) sufficiently close to \(a\), without considering \(x=a\).

Left-Hand Limit

We say \[ \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = L \] if we can make \(f(x)\) as close to \(L\) as desired for all \(x<a\) sufficiently close to \(a\), without considering \(x=a\).

Observation

The only difference between one-sided limits and ordinary limits is the direction from which \(x\) approaches \(a\). For a one-sided limit to exist, the function must approach a unique value from that side.

Examples

Example 35

Estimate: \[ \lim_{t \to 0^+} H(t) \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{t \to 0^-} H(t), \quad H(t)=\begin{cases}0,& t<0\\1,& t\ge 0\end{cases} \]

Solution: \[ \lim_{t \to 0^+} H(t) = 1, \quad \lim_{t \to 0^-} H(t) = 0 \]

Example 36

Estimate: \[ \lim_{t \to 0^+} \sin\left(\frac{1}{t}\right) \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{t \to 0^-} \sin\left(\frac{1}{t}\right) \]

Observation: The function oscillates infinitely near \(t=0\), so the one-sided limits do not exist.

Example 37

Let \[ g(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{x^2 + 5x -18}{x^2 -3x}, & x\neq 3\\ 7, & x=3 \end{cases} \]

Estimate: \[ \lim_{x \to 3^+} g(x) \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to 3^-} g(x) \]

Solution: Both one-sided limits exist and equal 4.

Fact Relating One-Sided and Ordinary Limits

Fact

For a function \(f(x)\):

  • If \(\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = L\), then \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L\).

  • Conversely, if \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L\), both one-sided limits exist and equal \(L\).

  • If \(\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) \neq \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)\), then \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\) does not exist.

Properties of Limits

Properties of Limits

Let \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x)\) and \(\lim_{x \to a} g(x)\) exist, and let \(c\) be any constant. Then the following properties hold:

1. Constant Multiple: \[ \lim_{x \to a} [c f(x)] = c \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \]

Example: \(\lim_{x \to 2} 4x^2 = 4 \cdot 4 = 16\)

2. Sum/Difference: \[ \lim_{x \to a} [f(x) \pm g(x)] = \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \pm \lim_{x \to a} g(x) \]

Example: \(\lim_{x \to 1} (x^2 + 3x) = 1 + 3 = 4\)

3. Product: \[ \lim_{x \to a} [f(x) g(x)] = \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \cdot \lim_{x \to a} g(x) \]

Example: \(\lim_{x \to 2} (x^2 \cdot (x+1)) = 4 \cdot 3 = 12\)

4. Quotient: \[ \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{\lim_{x \to a} f(x)}{\lim_{x \to a} g(x)}, \quad \lim_{x \to a} g(x) \neq 0 \]

Example: \(\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2-4}{x-2} = \lim_{x \to 2} (x+2) = 4\)

5. Power: \[ \lim_{x \to a} [f(x)]^n = [\lim_{x \to a} f(x)]^n \]

Example: \(\lim_{x \to 3} (x-1)^3 = 2^3 = 8\)

6. Root: \[ \lim_{x \to a} \sqrt[n]{f(x)} = \sqrt[n]{\lim_{x \to a} f(x)} \]

Example: \(\lim_{x \to 9} \sqrt{x} = 3\)

7. Constant Function: \(\lim_{x \to a} c = c\)

8. Identity Function: \(\lim_{x \to a} x = a\)

9. Power of Identity: \(\lim_{x \to a} x^n = a^n\)

Infinite Limits

In this section we study limits whose value is \(\infty\) or \(-\infty\). These limits appear frequently in calculus and other exams, so understanding them is essential.

Definition of Infinite Limits

We say \[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \infty \] if we can make \(f(x)\) arbitrarily large for all \(x\) sufficiently close to \(a\) (from both sides) without actually letting \(x = a\).

Similarly, \[ \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = -\infty \] if we can make \(f(x)\) arbitrarily large and negative for all \(x\) sufficiently close to \(a\) without letting \(x = a\).

These definitions can be extended to one-sided limits as well.

Vertical Asymptote

A function \(f(x)\) has a vertical asymptote at \(x = a\) if any of the following limits is true: \[ \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \pm \infty, \quad \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = \pm \infty, \quad \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \pm \infty \]

Note: Only one of the above needs to hold.

Facts About Infinite Limits

Let \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) be functions such that \[ \lim_{x \to c} f(x) = \infty, \quad \lim_{x \to c} g(x) = L \]

Then: \[ \lim_{x \to c} [f(x) \pm g(x)] = \infty \] \[ \lim_{x \to c} [f(x) g(x)] = \begin{cases} \infty & \text{if } L > 0\\ -\infty & \text{if } L < 0 \end{cases} \] \[ \lim_{x \to c} \frac{g(x)}{f(x)} = 0 \]

Example 38

Evaluate the following limits: \[ \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1}{x}, \quad \lim_{x \to 0^-} \frac{1}{x}, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x} \]

Solution: - As \(x \to 0^+\), \(x>0\) so \(\frac{1}{x} \to +\infty\). - As \(x \to 0^-\), \(x<0\) so \(\frac{1}{x} \to -\infty\). - Since the left and right limits are not equal, \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x}\) does not exist.

Example 39

Evaluate the following limits: \[ \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{6}{x^2}, \quad \lim_{x \to 0^-} \frac{6}{x^2}, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{6}{x^2} \]

Solution: - As \(x \to 0\), \(x^2>0\) so \(\frac{6}{x^2} \to +\infty\) from both sides. - Therefore, \(\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{6}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0^-} \frac{6}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{6}{x^2} = +\infty\)

Example 40

Evaluate the following limits: \[ \lim_{x \to -2^+} \frac{-4}{x+2}, \quad \lim_{x \to -2^-} \frac{-4}{x+2}, \quad \lim_{x \to -2} \frac{-4}{x+2} \]

Solution: - As \(x \to -2^+\), \(x+2>0\) so \(\frac{-4}{x+2} \to -\infty\). - As \(x \to -2^-\), \(x+2<0\) so \(\frac{-4}{x+2} \to +\infty\). - Left and right limits are not equal, so the limit at \(x=-2\) does not exist.

Example 41

Evaluate the following limits: \[ \lim_{x \to 4^+} \frac{3}{(4-x)^3}, \quad \lim_{x \to 4^-} \frac{3}{(4-x)^3}, \quad \lim_{x \to 4} \frac{3}{(4-x)^3} \]

Solution: - As \(x \to 4^+\), \(4-x < 0\), so \((4-x)^3 <0\) and \(\frac{3}{(4-x)^3} \to -\infty\). - As \(x \to 4^-\), \(4-x >0\), so \(\frac{3}{(4-x)^3} \to +\infty\). - Since left and right limits differ, \(\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{3}{(4-x)^3}\) does not exist.

Example 42

Evaluate the following limits: \[ \lim_{x \to 3^+} \frac{2x}{x-3}, \quad \lim_{x \to 3^-} \frac{2x}{x-3}, \quad \lim_{x \to 3} \frac{2x}{x-3} \]

Solution: - As \(x \to 3^+\), \(x-3>0\) so \(\frac{2x}{x-3} \to +\infty\). - As \(x \to 3^-\), \(x-3<0\) so \(\frac{2x}{x-3} \to -\infty\). - Left and right limits differ, so \(\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{2x}{x-3}\) does not exist.

Example 43

Evaluate the one-sided limit: \[ \lim_{x \to 0^+} \ln x \]

Solution: - As \(x \to 0^+\), \(\ln x \to -\infty\). - So, \(\lim_{x \to 0^+} \ln x = -\infty\)

Example 44

Evaluate the following limits: \[ \lim_{x \to \pi/2^+} \tan x, \quad \lim_{x \to \pi/2^-} \tan x \]

Solution: - As \(x \to \pi/2^-\), \(\tan x \to +\infty\) - As \(x \to \pi/2^+\), \(\tan x \to -\infty\)

Example 45

Show that a vertical asymptote exists at \(x=0\) for \(f(x) = 1/x\).

Solution: - One-sided limits: \(\lim_{x\to 0^+} 1/x = +\infty\), \(\lim_{x\to 0^-} 1/x = -\infty\) - Since at least one one-sided limit goes to \(\pm\infty\), \(x=0\) is a vertical asymptote.

Example 46

Evaluate the following limit: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x+1}{x^2} \]

Solution: - Split: \(\frac{5x+1}{x^2} = \frac{5x}{x^2} + \frac{1}{x^2} = \frac{5}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2}\) - As \(x \to 0^+\), \(\frac{5}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} \to +\infty\) - As \(x \to 0^-\), \(\frac{5}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} \to -\infty + \infty = +\infty\) (dominant term \(1/x^2\)) - So, \(\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{5x+1}{x^2} = +\infty\)

Example 47

Evaluate the following limit: \[ \lim_{x \to -1^+} \frac{2}{x+1}, \quad \lim_{x \to -1^-} \frac{2}{x+1} \]

Solution: - As \(x \to -1^+\), \(x+1>0 \implies \frac{2}{x+1} \to +\infty\) - As \(x \to -1^-\), \(x+1<0 \implies \frac{2}{x+1} \to -\infty\)

Limits at Infinity

By limits at infinity, we study the behavior of functions as \(x\) becomes very large: \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) \quad \text{or} \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) \] These limits may also be \(\infty\) or \(-\infty\).

Fact 1: Constant over Power

If \(r > 0\) is rational and \(c\) is a constant: \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{c}{x^r} = 0, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{c}{x^r} = 0 \quad (\text{if } x^r \text{ defined for } x<0) \] Explanation: As \(x \to \infty\), \(x^r\) grows, making the fraction approach 0. The sign of \(c\) only affects the approach from above or below.

Fact 2: Polynomials at Infinity

For \(p(x) = a_n x^n + \dots + a_0\), degree \(n\), \(a_n \neq 0\): \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} p(x) = \lim_{x \to \infty} a_n x^n, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} p(x) = \lim_{x \to -\infty} a_n x^n \] Explanation: Highest-degree term dominates; lower-degree terms are negligible.

Horizontal Asymptote

A function \(f(x)\) has a horizontal asymptote at \(y = L\) if: \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L \quad \text{or} \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = L \] Explanation: The graph approaches \(y=L\) as \(x\) moves far from the origin.

Example 48

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} (2x^4 - x^2 - 8x), \quad \lim_{t \to -\infty} \left(\frac{1}{3} t^5 + 2 t^3 - t^2 + 8 \right) \]

Solution:

  • \(\lim_{x \to \infty} (2x^4 - x^2 - 8x) = \infty\) (dominant \(2x^4\)).

  • \(\lim_{t \to -\infty} (\frac{1}{3} t^5 + \dots) = -\infty\) (dominant \(\frac{1}{3}t^5\) negative).

Example 49

\[ \lim_{x \to \pm \infty} \frac{2x^4 - x^2 + 8x}{-5x^4 + 7} \]

Solution: Factor \(x^4\): \[ \frac{2 - 1/x^2 + 8/x^3}{-5 + 7/x^4} \to -\frac{2}{5} \quad \text{as } x \to \pm \infty \]

Example 50

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\sqrt{3x^2 + 6}}{5 - 2x}, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{\sqrt{3x^2 + 6}}{5 - 2x} \]

Solution:

  • \(x \to \infty: \frac{\sqrt{x^2(3 + 6/x^2)}}{x(-2 + 5/x)} \to -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)

  • \(x \to -\infty: \frac{-x\sqrt{3 + 6/x^2}}{x(-2 + 5/x)} \to \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\)

Exponential Function Behavior

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} e^x = \infty, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} e^x = 0, \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} e^{-x} = 0, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} e^{-x} = \infty \]

Example 51

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} e^{2 - 4x - 8x^2} = 0, \quad \lim_{t \to -\infty} e^{t^4 - 5 t^2 + 1} = \infty, \quad \lim_{z \to 0^+} e^{1/z} = \infty \]

Example 52

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \left(e^{10x} - 4 e^{6x} + \dots \right) = \infty, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} \left(e^{10x} - 4 e^{6x} + \dots \right) = -\infty \]

Logarithmic Function Limits

\[ \lim_{x \to 0^+} \ln x = -\infty, \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} \ln x = \infty \]

Example 53

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \ln(7x^3 - x^2 + 1) = \infty, \quad \lim_{t \to -\infty} \ln(t^2 - 5t) = \infty \]

Inverse Tangent Function Limits

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \tan^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} \tan^{-1} x = -\frac{\pi}{2} \]

Example 54

\[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \tan^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} \tan^{-1} x = -\frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \lim_{x \to \infty} \tan^{-1}(x^3 - 5x + 6) = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad \lim_{x \to 0^-} \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = -\frac{\pi}{2} \]

Limit Formulas

Basic Limit Properties

  • $\lim_{x \to a} c = c$

  • $\lim_{x \to a} x = a$

  • $\lim_{x \to a} \big(f(x) \pm g(x)\big) = \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \pm \lim_{x \to a} g(x)$

  • $\lim_{x \to a} \big(f(x) g(x)\big) = \lim_{x \to a} f(x) \cdot \lim_{x \to a} g(x)$

  • $\lim_{x \to a} \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \dfrac{\lim_{x \to a} f(x)}{\lim_{x \to a} g(x)}, \; g(a)\neq 0$

Limits Involving Infinity (Rational Functions)

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{a_n x^n + \cdots}{b_m x^m + \cdots} = \begin{cases} 0, & n < m \\ \dfrac{a_n}{b_m}, & n = m \\ \infty \text{ or } -\infty, & n > m \end{cases}$

  • $\lim_{x \to a^-} \dfrac{1}{x-a} = -\infty$

  • $\lim_{x \to a^+} \dfrac{1}{x-a} = +\infty$

Standard Trigonometric Limits

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin x}{x} = 1$

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\tan x}{x} = 1$

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Exponential and Logarithmic Limits

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{e^x - 1}{x} = 1$

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\ln(1+x)}{x} = 1$

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{x}\right)^x = e$

  • $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^n \ln x = 0 \quad (n>0)$

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{\ln x}{x} = 0$

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{x^n}{e^x} = 0$

Inverse Trigonometric Limits

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} \arctan x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

  • $\lim_{x \to -\infty} \arctan x = -\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\arcsin x}{x} = 1$

  • $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\arctan x}{x} = 1$

L'Hospital's Rule

  • If $\lim_{x \to a} \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ is $\dfrac{0}{0}$ or $\dfrac{\infty}{\infty}$, then \[ \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}, \quad \text{if the limit exists.} \]

Squeeze Theorem

  • If $f(x) \le g(x) \le h(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L$, then $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = L$

Other Useful Limits

  • $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^x = 1$

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \frac{k}{x}\right)^{mx} = e^{km}, \quad k,m \in \mathbb{R}$

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} e^{-x} = 0$

  • $\lim_{x \to -\infty} e^x = 0$

  • $\lim_{x \to \infty} e^x = \infty$

  • $\lim_{x \to -\infty} e^{-x} = \infty$

Example 55

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 5x - 1) \).

Solution: Using the sum and constant multiple properties of limits: \[ \lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 + 5x - 1) = 3 \lim_{x \to 2} x^2 + 5 \lim_{x \to 2} x - 1 = 3(4) + 5(2) -1 = 21 \]

Example 56

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to -1} \frac{x^3 + 1}{x+1} \).

Solution: Factor numerator: \(x^3+1=(x+1)(x^2-x+1)\) \[ \lim_{x \to -1} \frac{(x+1)(x^2-x+1)}{x+1} = \lim_{x \to -1} (x^2-x+1) = 3 \]

Example 57

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{x+4} - 2}{x} \).

Solution: Rationalize the numerator: \[ \frac{\sqrt{x+4}-2}{x} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x+4}+2}{\sqrt{x+4}+2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2} \implies \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4} \]

Example 58

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2-1}{x-1} \).

Solution: Factor numerator: \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\) \[ \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1} = \lim_{x \to 1} (x+1) = 2 \]

Example 59

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1-\cos x}{x^2} \) using \(1-\cos x = 2\sin^2(x/2)\).

Solution: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2\sin^2(x/2)}{x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} 2 \left(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x}\right)^2 = 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \]

Example 60

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 4} \sqrt{x} \).

Solution: Using the root property: \[ \lim_{x \to 4} \sqrt{x} = \sqrt{\lim_{x \to 4} x} = 2 \]

Example 61

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{x} \).

Solution: Factor 3: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{x} = 3 \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 3x}{3x} = 3(1) = 3 \]

Example 62

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} \).

Solution: Standard limit property: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1 \]

Example 63

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^4-1}{x-1} \).

Solution: Factor numerator: \(x^4-1 = (x-1)(x^3+x^2+x+1)\) \[ \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{(x-1)(x^3+x^2+x+1)}{x-1} = \lim_{x \to 1} (x^3+x^2+x+1) = 4 \]

Example 64

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2-4}{x^2+4} \).

Solution: Factor numerator: \(x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)\) \[ \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x^2+4} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2} \]

Example 65

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x-1}{x} \).

Solution: Standard exponential limit: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x-1}{x} = 1 \]

Example 66

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+x)}{x} \).

Solution: Standard logarithmic limit: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln(1+x)}{x} = 1 \]

Example 67

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{\sqrt{x}-1}{x-1} \).

Solution: Rationalize numerator: \[ \frac{\sqrt{x}-1}{x-1} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x}+1}{\sqrt{x}+1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}+1} \implies \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \]

Example 68

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x + \tan x}{x} \).

Solution: Split limit: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} + \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = 1 + 1 = 2 \]

Example 69

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0^+} \ln x \).

Solution: As \(x \to 0^+\), \(\ln x \to -\infty\)

Example 70

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^2+2}{5x^2-7} \).

Solution: Divide numerator and denominator by \(x^2\): \[ \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3+2/x^2}{5-7/x^2} = \frac{3}{5} \]

Example 71

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x} \).

Solution: Limit does not exist; as \(x \to 0^+\), it \(\to \infty\); as \(x \to 0^-\), it \(\to -\infty\)

Example 72

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 2x}{\sin 3x} \).

Solution: Rewrite: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2x}{3x} \cdot \frac{\sin 2x/(2x)}{\sin 3x/(3x)} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1 = \frac{2}{3} \]

Example 73

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 1} (x^5 - 1) \).

Solution: Direct substitution: \[ \lim_{x \to 1} (x^5-1) = 1-1=0 \]

Example 74

Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{2x}-1}{\sin x} \).

Solution: Split and use standard limits: \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^{2x}-1}{x} \cdot \frac{x}{\sin x} = 2 \cdot 1 = 2 \]
