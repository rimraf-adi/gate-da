Verbal aptitude tests how well you read, write and reason with English. Each paper has about five such questions, usually grammar, vocabulary, sentence completion or short reading comprehension. Accuracy comes from knowing a small set of rules and from careful elimination.

## Grammar essentials

**Subject-verb agreement**
- The verb agrees with the **subject**, not the noun nearest to it: *The box of chocolates **is** on the table.*
- *Each, every, either, neither, one of, none (of one)* take a singular verb: *Neither of the boys **is** here.*
- Subjects joined by **and** are plural; with **or / nor** the verb agrees with the nearer subject: *Neither the teacher nor the students **are** present.*
- Collective nouns (team, committee) are singular when acting as one unit.

**Tenses and their common traps**
- Do not use *will* in a time clause: *I will call you when he **arrives*** (not *will arrive*).
- Use the **past perfect** for the earlier of two past events: *He had left before I **reached**.*
- *If* clauses: real conditions use *if + present, will*; unreal ones use *if + past, would* (*If I **were** you, I would go*).

**Pronouns, articles, prepositions**
- Pronoun case after prepositions is objective: *between **you and me*** (not *you and I*).
- *Fewer* for countable nouns, *less* for uncountable: *fewer mistakes, less water*.
- *A* before a consonant **sound**, *an* before a vowel sound: *a university, an hour*.
- Fixed prepositions: *different from*, *prefer X to Y*, *capable of*, *interested in*, *good at*, *afraid of*, *married to*, *at 5 o'clock, on Monday, in June*.

**Parallelism and modifiers.** Items in a list must have the same form: *She likes **reading, writing and painting*** (not *to paint*). Place modifiers next to what they describe.

## Vocabulary

**Word roots and prefixes** unlock unfamiliar words: *bene-* good (benefit), *mal-* bad (malice), *anti-* against, *pre-* before, *post-* after, *dis-/un-* not, *-phobia* fear, *auto-* self, *chrono-* time.

**Commonly confused words**

| Pair | Meaning |
|---|---|
| affect / effect | to influence / a result |
| principal / principle | head or main / a rule |
| complement / compliment | completes / praise |
| stationary / stationery | not moving / writing material |
| council / counsel | a body / advice |
| ensure / insure | make certain / cover with insurance |
| elicit / illicit | draw out / unlawful |

**Phrasal verbs often tested**

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| call off | cancel |
| put up with | tolerate |
| look into | investigate |
| turn down | reject |
| carry out | perform |
| give in | surrender |
| bring about | cause |
| come across | find by chance |

**Analogies.** Work out the exact relationship in the first pair, then apply it: *Doctor : Patient :: Teacher : Student* (serves). Typical relations: part and whole, cause and effect, tool and user, degree (*warm : hot*), synonym, antonym.

## Sentence completion

1. Read the sentence and predict the missing idea **before** looking at the options.
2. Look for **clue words**: contrast (*but, although, despite, however*) flips the meaning; support (*and, moreover, therefore*) keeps it.
3. Check grammar and register, then eliminate extreme or off-tone options.

:::example Using clue words
*Although the critics were harsh, the film was a ______ success.* The word *although* signals contrast with *harsh*, so the blank needs a positive word such as *commercial* or *resounding*, not *disastrous*.
:::

## Para jumbles

- The **opening sentence** introduces the topic and has no pronoun pointing back (*he, this, it, they*).
- Find **mandatory pairs**: a sentence containing *this, such, however* must follow the one it refers to; a definition follows its term.
- Look for connectors and logic: general to specific, cause then effect, chronological order.
- The **concluding sentence** often summarises or looks forward.

## Reading comprehension

- Read the questions first, then skim the passage for the **main idea**: the topic sentence of each paragraph.
- Distinguish **fact** (stated), **inference** (follows from the text) and **opinion** (the author's view). Answer inference questions only from the passage, never from outside knowledge.
- Be wary of options with extreme words (*always, never, only, completely*).
- For **tone** or **purpose** questions, look at word choice: *critical, sceptical, neutral, optimistic*.
- For a word-in-context question, substitute each option back into the sentence.

:::example Fact versus inference
Passage: *Cities that cut bus fares saw ridership rise, but traffic on the same roads did not fall.* An **inference** that fits is that cheaper fares alone did not reduce car use. A claim that *buses are unpopular* is not supported.
:::

## What the exam asks

- Choose the grammatically correct sentence or fill a blank with the right word, tense or preposition.
- Pick the best **synonym, antonym or analogy**.
- Complete a sentence using clue words, or order jumbled sentences.
- Answer a few questions on a short passage, mostly **inference and main idea**.
